# Testing

Automated checks come first, then in-game checks. A screenshot from one flight shows the setup it tested, not every map or aircraft.

## Automated

```powershell
dotnet run --project tests/NoMapOverhaul.Tests -c Release
```

- received drawings: unit circles and notes placed on the recipient's tracks, unknown versus stale anchor visibility, received drawings split evenly between teammates with distinct IDs for hidden drawings, stable remote drawing IDs, content and order changes, owner isolation, and reset isolation;
- budgets and floods: received-snapshot cadence, change costs including ID rotation, budget refill and deferral that still delivers the newest snapshot, the per-peer byte window, a simulated ten-second attack of changing 100-note snapshots with rotating IDs at 5 Hz, a stall's backlog received up to the flood limit, receive and nonce churn budgets surviving a restart, and quarantine surviving a timeout and a roster rejoin, with its 64-ID stop condition and local reset;
- sending: the oldest drawings that fit when a snapshot is over them, snapshot retries, three consecutive send failures and the exact 30-second retry deadline, nonce rotation, heartbeats reporting the newest snapshot a teammate holds, and unchanged heartbeat references and versions;
- pairing: faction and mission epochs, simultaneous and repeated handshakes, a heartbeat Hello crossing a one-sided restart, sparse probes to teammates not yet known to run the mod that channel changes don't renew, a teammate whose Hello arrives after the probes ran out pairing despite a lost acknowledgement, late incoming handshakes after three exhausted capability attempts, recovery from a dropped confirming acknowledgement, reconnect and expired-peer replay rejection, and a full teammate roster told apart from invalid peer data;
- editing and erasing: edit requests limited to drawing IDs actually sent and to both players' channel lists, both broadcasters required for collaboration, peers built read-only by default, receive-only delivery without publishing or erasure, shared BRA erase permission revoked and restored, and erase eligibility during cooldown and sequence exhaustion.

BRA rendering tests pin map and HUD measurements to positions the receiver provides. Received arrows read lost at the last known position and hide only for a unit never known, and local lost-track labels stay. Teammates' drawings stack in the order the teammates first sent them, through edits, until one pairs again. These tests don't run Unity's faction lookup. Scheduling tests pin the 200 ms deadline, five polling ticks across 120 frames, and no catch-up burst after a stall. The received-drawing store accepts 32 owners and rejects a 33rd. The wire format accepts five 50-waypoint routes and rejects a sixth route or a 51st waypoint.

The reused encode and receive buffers keep each packet's bytes apart and reject a length past the packet or the buffer. The allocation-free channel-name check agrees with configuring that name. A snapshot readdressed to another teammate matches one encoded for them byte for byte. A teammate's unchanged drawings come back as the same objects from one snapshot to the next, never across teammates' sessions and never once shown on the map. With that cache primed, truncated and randomly damaged frames decode or fail exactly as without it, and records spliced from it still fail the snapshot's point cap and duplicate-ID checks. A receive test double pins 32-message native batches, the 128-message global window, exhaustion leaving a one-shot erase queued without releasing it, partial-window dequeue sizes, and reset.

After restoring the test project, run the focused sharing and collaborative erase tests:

```powershell
pwsh ./tools/Test-Sharing.ps1
```

The script builds first and stops at the first failure. It runs the game-free state logic, not native Steam sends, receive queues, or live roster admission.

Network-log tests pin silence while disabled, invariant timestamps, 128 event lines per five seconds, suppression counts flushed during idle updates, no catch-up windows after a stall, and disabling and re-enabling. They run the log limiter without Steam or Unity.

Steam callback ownership tests reject foreign outgoing and incoming handles, including a second connection to the same peer, and invalid handles. They keep the gameplay listener's full connection lifecycle, and its terminal cleanup when listener metadata is missing. Recovery tests pin an accepted send that later fails asynchronously, duplicate callbacks, the exact 30-second deadline, one probe per mission to an unknown peer across failures, an incoming Hello during backoff, and recovery for a peer that was compatible before.

After building the plugin, this script checks the installed Mirage callback signatures and fields, then calls the compiled ownership prefixes with synthetic Steam handles:

```powershell
pwsh ./tools/Test-SteamConnectionOwnership.ps1
```

Its seven offline cases cover owned, foreign, invalid, and terminal callbacks. It makes no native Steam calls and applies no Harmony detours, since installing the patches needs the game's Mono runtime.

The suite doesn't start Unity or the game. It doesn't cover drawing, map scale, font borrowing, input handling, 3D label stacking, the perf test switching the mod and map, or game-version compatibility.

## Automated in-game checks for 0.6.0 beta 4

NO-Agentic-Framework ran these on 2026-10-22, game build 24724372. They add evidence, but the owner's own check of how things look still decides.

- **3D rings on the GPU.** Paused frames at 150 m past ten ships with the heavy drawings, flying straight and weaving. Each frame was shot with the shader and with the CPU path (RingsOnCpu) at the same moment. Seven of eight frames differ by 2 to 38 pixels in 3.7 million. The eighth, with many rings edge-on at the horizon, differs by 1,690. Rings cut at the screen edge and the near plane alike, hide while the full map is open, and the log names the path in use. The shader loads from the plugin folder.
- **Batched notes.** The same flight and perf-test drawings, shot with notes batched and with one TextMeshPro object per note, on the minimap and full map at the same slice. The notes read the same, rims and colours included, and stand upright on the heading-up minimap. The mod's graphics fell from 138 to 99, and its TextMeshPro objects from 39 to 19.
- **Rail with custom colours.** At 2560x1440 and 1920x1080, with two custom colours saved, the rail stood in three columns left of the map. Its top sat about 14 design pixels above the map's and clear of the speed readout, and its bottom cleared the BDF button. Add on a colour that the built-in green swatch or the custom purple one already had picked that swatch and saved nothing. Add on a new colour still saved it in the next slot.
- **Four players sharing.** Four game instances on one PC ran the loopback build (`build/Build-Loopback.ps1`) for 15 minutes, each running the Bidirectional perf test with 71 drawings and a note changing twice a second. There was no decline across 32 cycles and no growth in graphics or heap. A pre-fix build under the same load didn't decline either, so the drop the owner reported in larger sessions is still unreproduced.
- **Performance log detail.** Two runs: single player flying level before, during, and after a perf test, then four loopback players each running the Bidirectional perf test. Every window logged its detail line. Outside the test it held the canvas update and render counters, about 0.5 ms and 285 batches a frame in single player and 0.65 ms and 300 batches on the group's host. While the test ran it held the perf test's own note, and the canvas time came back once the test ended. Rebuild counts rose with the test's drawings. Rings read up to 16 on the GPU, and not drawn while the full map was open. The context read 1 on the team in single player and 4 in the group. The host's teammates line named three ready peers, each holding and showing 71 drawings at the test's heavy slices, with their messages and kilobytes each way and `Steam n/a` on the loopback transport. Steam's session status wasn't checked between two PCs. Both runs passed again once each window started before its lines were written: 19 windows in single player, and 35 to 46 per player in the group. Each window had its detail line, every window had a teammates line once sharing paired, mission time read in multiplayer, and the log raised no warning.

## Local 0.6.0 preview

A two-player Steam playtest on 2026-10-05 reported missing drawings and unreliable channel changes. A repeat test with diagnostics found the same protocol and assembly build on both clients, 20 outgoing Hello attempts on the host and 29 on the other client, no completed handshake, and no exchanged snapshots. The host's game log twice records Mirage trying to accept a Steam Messages connection and getting InvalidParam just before the drawing session fails. The installed Mirage transport's callbacks had no connection ownership checks. The compatibility patch filters those callbacks, and asynchronous failures now trigger bounded recovery.
